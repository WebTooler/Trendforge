## Funding round and founding story

Comp AI, a cybersecurity and compliance startup, announced a $34 million Series A round led by Roo Capital and Grand Ventures. The company was founded last January by Lewis Carhart, Claudio Fuentes and Mariano Fuentes. Carhart now serves as CEO, while Claudio Fuentes holds the COO role and Mariano Fuentes is CTO. The founders previously operated a startup for about two years that reached over one million users before shutting down because it lacked a sticky enough use case. That experience taught them how to build with large language models and the importance of a clear product focus. In the new venture, Carhart leads as CEO because it was his idea.

## Agentic platform and its role in compliance

Comp AI is building an agentic platform that uses AI agents to draft company security policies and collect evidence for security audits. The platform also provides continuous monitoring to verify that a company meets its compliance controls. Carhart explained that for many software firms, security and compliance are directly tied to revenue, often required before closing deals such as a SOC 2 report. He said Comp AI automates much of the traditional work around that process but does not replace independent audit reviews. To date, the company has raised $37.5 million in total funding.

## Market context and why continuous monitoring matters

The broader market is seeing a new wave of security risks in the agentic era, prompting the emergence of AI security and compliance firms like Vanta and Drata. Carhart noted that rapid adoption and experimentation with AI are creating demand for continuous, possibly autonomous, security and compliance solutions. Comp AI is addressing this by starting with permissions and accountability, aiming to build a security layer that continuously monitors and validates risk as AI systems evolve.

## Limitations and what readers should watch

 The company’s previous shutdown highlights the challenge of finding a durable use case, and the current approach still depends on human audit review for final validation. Readers should watch for details on how the AI agents handle complex regulatory requirements and whether the platform can scale beyond early adopters.
