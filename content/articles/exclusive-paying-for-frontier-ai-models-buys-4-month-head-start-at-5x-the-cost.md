## The shrinking frontier between open and closed AI
A Mozilla report released in September 2026 shows that the performance gap between leading U.S. frontier AI models and the best open‑weight Chinese models has narrowed to roughly 4.4 months. The same report, which Ars previewed before publication, also found that eight of the top ten models by token volume in August 2026 are open‑weight, indicating a shift in usage patterns. Despite this progress, open models still trail closed frontier models in revenue, suggesting that commercial adoption lags behind technical capability.

## How the cost gap is reshaping decisions
For most workloads, organizations should default to open models, according to Mozilla’s State of Open Source AI report published on September 15. The report highlighted Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3, which scores just three points below Anthropic’s Fable 5 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index while costing only 30 % of the closed model’s price. Mozilla CTO Raffi Krikorian explained that closed models retain value in expert professional tasks, high‑intensity retrieval, and long‑context scenarios. He added that closed models come with compliance packaging, support, and accountability, which many firms lack the staff to replicate with open‑weight systems.

DoorDash illustrates the emerging practice, using Kimi for routine work and reserving Fable for more complex problems that would otherwise require human experts. The cost advantage is stark: the open‑weight GLM 5.2 from Z.ai scored within a point of Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.7 and 4.8 in Terminal‑Bench 2.1 testing, yet costs about five times less per completed task. Paying for closed frontier models buys about a four‑month head start at about five times the per-task cost—but only when currently looking at tasks taking between eight and 12 hours.

Krikorian advised that the premium is justified only when the head start matters, such as meeting a deadline before open models catch up. For routine work that will still be performed next quarter, he argued, the savings of using open models—potentially a fifth of the cost—outweigh any performance edge. Since Mozilla’s inaugural State of Open Source AI report appeared on July 14, adoption of open models has accelerated, reflecting both cost savings and the narrowing capability gap.

## Where closed models still hold an edge
Even as the gap closes, closed frontier models retain advantages in regulated environments and scenarios where immediate out‑of‑the‑box performance is critical. Organizations still pay for closed frontier models because they work out of the box and come bundled with compliance packaging, support, and accountability, whereas many organizations lack the staff to run open‑weight models well, Krikorian explained.
