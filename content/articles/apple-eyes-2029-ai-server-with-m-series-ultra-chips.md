## A Long‑Abandoned Line Re‑Enters the Spotlight
Apple has been absent from the enterprise server market for decades, and the planned 2029 server could be its first enterprise server in decades. According to a report from Ars Technica, the company is now working on a new AI server that would use Apple’s high‑performance M‑Series Ultra chips found in Mac desktops. The projected release of this product in 2029 would mark Apple’s first server to hit the market in almost two decades.

The move would also come after a surge in demand for Apple hardware from AI developers. The report notes that companies such as OpenAI have purchased “tens of thousands” of Mac minis and Mac Studios to train AI agents through trial‑and‑error reinforcement learning, while Anthropic has rented Mac minis from Amazon Web Services.

## What the Server Could Look Like
The Information, which has been closely monitoring Apple’s plans, reports that the future enterprise server would come in two configurations that include either two or four of Apple’s future M8 Ultra chips. Apple is also considering connecting its M8 chips using data‑center networking equipment from Nvidia.

Specifically, the potential use of Nvidia’s NVLink Fusion technology has been discussed in conversations between Apple and Nvidia. The Information cautions that the project could still be canceled or proceed without incorporating Nvidia’s technology.

## Leadership and Timing
The project reportedly received support from new Apple CEO John Ternus when it began a year ago, back when Ternus led Apple’s hardware engineering efforts. Ternus officially took over as Apple CEO from Tim Cook on September 1, after the latter had served in the leadership position for 15 years. If Apple’s AI server eventually comes to fruition, it could be a notable part of John Ternus’ legacy.

## Supply‑Chain Bottleneck
Apple’s server plans and the growing popularity of Mac computers during the AI boom also face a notable supply‑chain challenge shared across the tech industry: the ongoing shortage of memory chips because of companies rushing to build AI data centers. As Ars has documented, the memory shortage has led to significant price increases for many consumer electronics such as gaming devices and many flagship smartphones, not to mention a wide range of Apple products.

## What Readers Should Watch
The next few years will reveal whether Apple’s server project advances, whether it incorporates Nvidia’s networking technology, and how the company navigates the memory‑chip shortage. The ongoing memory-chip shortage is a notable supply-chain challenge for Apple's server plans.

For now, the information points to a potential return of Apple to the enterprise server market, driven by its M‑Series Ultra chips and the AI industry’s demand for powerful, Apple‑based computing platforms.
