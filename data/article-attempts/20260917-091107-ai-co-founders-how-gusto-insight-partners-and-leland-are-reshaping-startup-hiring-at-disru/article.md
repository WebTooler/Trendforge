## Redefining Early‑Stage Hiring with AI Co‑Founders
The session titled “Hiring When AI Is a Co‑Founder” will take place at TechCrunch Disrupt 2026. It features Josh Reeves, CEO and co‑founder of Gusto; Michelle Johnson, senior vice president at Insight Partners; and John Koelliker, CEO and co‑founder of Leland. The discussion focuses on how early‑stage startups can build teams where humans and AI agents collaborate without sacrificing speed, accountability, or culture. Disrupt 2026 runs October 13–15 at Moscone West in San Francisco, gathering more than 10,000 startups, investors, and tech decision‑makers. Attendees can register before September 25 at 11:59 p.m. to receive a discount of up to $200 on a pass, and group registrations add up to 30 % extra savings.

## How Gusto, Insight Partners, and Leland See the Shift
For an early‑stage company, each new hire represents a trade‑off among capability, cost, and speed. Engineering work, customer support, and operational tasks are increasingly delegated to systems that can complete multi‑step processes on their own. Michelle Johnson advises CEOs and CROs across North America and Europe on go‑to‑market strategy, revenue organization, and AI implementation. Before joining Insight, she helped scale Flock Safety from less than $1 million to $90 million in annual recurring revenue while leading early sales and revenue operations. Founders still need people who can make decisions, take responsibility, understand customers, and shape the company culture. The central challenge is determining where human judgment adds the most value and where an AI agent can perform the role instead.

## Practical Tasks AI Can Take Over Today
Evidence from the panel shows that engineering tasks, customer support, and operational work can be handed to AI systems capable of handling multistep workflows. This delegation is presented as a way to accelerate execution and reduce early‑stage expenses. The speakers illustrate how AI can act as a co‑founder by taking on repetitive or data‑intensive responsibilities, freeing human team members to focus on strategic decision‑making and cultural stewardship.

## What Founders Still Need to Own
Analysis: While AI can execute defined processes, the session emphasizes that founders must retain ownership of vision, customer empathy, and cultural leadership. Human leaders are still required to interpret ambiguous market signals, negotiate partnerships, and build trust with stakeholders. The panel suggests that the most valuable human contributions lie in areas where judgment, creativity, and relational intelligence are essential—domains where AI currently lacks depth.

## What to Watch at Disrupt 2026
The three speakers will continue the conversation with the broader Disrupt audience across the Expo Hall, Startup Battlefield 200, and numerous networking sessions. Attendees can explore how to design hybrid teams, ask questions about AI integration workflows, and connect with peers facing similar hiring dilemmas. The session is categorized under AI, Startups, and TechCrunch Disrupt, signaling its relevance to founders seeking practical guidance on the next wave of workforce evolution.
