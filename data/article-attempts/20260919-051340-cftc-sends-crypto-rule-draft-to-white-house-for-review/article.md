## A Preliminary Step in Federal Crypto Oversight
The Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) has submitted a new regulatory action to the White House’s Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs. The filing, identified by RIN 3038‑AF80 and titled “Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions and Regulation Crypto Asset Markets,” is currently listed as a prerule on Reginfo.gov and is not deemed economically significant.

This move follows the Senate’s failure to advance the CLARITY Act, a piece of legislation that would have expanded the CFTC’s authority over digital commodities. The CFTC’s filing is the first visible regulatory step after the 49‑50 cloture vote that left the existing division of authority between federal regulators largely unchanged.

## What a Prerule Means for the Process
A prerule designation indicates that the agency is still exploring whether and how to begin formal rulemaking. The CFTC has not yet released a proposed rule for public comment. According to Reginfo.gov, the prerule stage is used to determine whether or how to begin rulemaking.

Because the action remains at this preliminary stage, the next milestone will be the release of additional documents that explain which markets and activities the CFTC intends to regulate.

## OIRA Review and Public Engagement
The White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) will review the CFTC action under Executive Order 12866. OIRA’s review allows the regulatory office to examine an agency action before it moves further through the rulemaking process. While the action is under review, members of the public can request meetings with OIRA to discuss the proposed regulations.

## A Broader Regulatory Context
The CFTC’s latest filing is part of a wider effort to clarify jurisdictional boundaries between the CFTC and the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). Both agencies have been working through a formal harmonization initiative intended to reduce overlapping requirements and develop a regulatory framework covering crypto assets and emerging market structures. Former CFTC Chair Christopher Giancarlo has indicated that the SEC and CFTC will continue to write crypto rules even without new market‑structure legislation this year.

## What Readers Should Watch
1. **Additional CFTC documents**: The release of more detailed guidance from the CFTC will clarify which markets and activities it plans to target.
2. **OIRA’s assessment**: The outcome of the White House review will indicate whether the agency action proceeds to formal rulemaking.
3. **Legislative developments**: Although the CLARITY Act did not pass, future congressional action could influence the regulatory trajectory.

Until those next steps occur, the CFTC’s filing remains an early, exploratory stage in the federal oversight of crypto markets.
