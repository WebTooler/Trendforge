## A New Legislative Turn on AI
On September 3, 2026, Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act. The proposal would permanently prohibit the development of artificial superintelligence in the United States and temporarily pause research on frontier models. It also calls for a federal Department of Artificial Intelligence to oversee advanced AI. The bill emerged after industry leaders urged Congress to impose guardrails on the technology.

## What the Act Would Ban
The legislation defines artificial superintelligence as a system that exceeds human cognitive ability or has sufficient capabilities to plan and execute the destruction or disempowerment of humanity. Under the bill, any entity that violates the ban could face up to twenty years in prison, while companies that break the law would face forced dissolution. The penalties mirror those for unlawfully developing nuclear weapons, underscoring the seriousness the sponsors attach to the risk.

## Industry Voices Behind the Proposal
OpenAI safety researcher Juan Felipe Cerón Uribe praised the bill, warning that superintelligence could go “extremely right or extremely wrong” and that “we shouldn’t be playing such games.” Google DeepMind engineer Swante Scholz echoed the concern, stating that the current path of development most likely leads to an existential catastrophe for humanity and that a ban would be a positive change for the foreseeable future. These remarks illustrate how some leading AI developers see the proposed law as a necessary check.

## Political Odds and International Reach
The bill faces long odds in a Republican‑controlled Congress that has struggled to unify even on less sweeping AI regulations. Even if the United States enacts the ban, the supply of AI models largely comes from American labs, meaning the measure would reshape what UK and EU users and developers can access. Thus, the act’s influence could extend beyond domestic borders, affecting global AI deployment.

## What’s Still Uncertain
While the bill sets a clear legal framework for banning superintelligence, it does not automatically alter existing AI governance frameworks. The measure’s practical impact on current AI projects and research institutions remains unclear, as does how quickly a Department of Artificial Intelligence could be established. Moreover, the bill’s success hinges on political negotiations that have not yet taken place.

## Watching the Next Steps
Readers should monitor congressional hearings and debates to gauge whether the act moves beyond the introduction stage. Pay attention to how industry stakeholders respond—whether they support, modify, or oppose the legislation—as well as any amendments that might broaden or narrow the definition of superintelligence. Finally, observe how international regulators react, as the U.S. ban could prompt similar measures elsewhere.

## What the Evidence Shows
The Senate and House bills present a concrete proposal to ban artificial superintelligence, create a federal AI agency, and punish violators with up to twenty years in prison. They reflect a growing consensus among some AI researchers that unchecked development poses an existential threat. Yet the bill’s passage depends on bipartisan cooperation and will likely influence global AI policy if enacted.
