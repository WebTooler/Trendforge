## San Francisco’s lawsuit
San Francisco’s city attorney is urging a state court to block Trump’s social media platform Truth Social from offering customers who pay $100,000 per month priority access to the president’s and other high‑level officials’ posts before they’re available to other users. The complaint says the $100,000‑per‑month Truth API lets customers view announcements that could move financial markets before they appear to other users, creating a financial advantage for the company and for President Trump, who owns 41 % of the company through a trust in which he is the sole beneficiary. The lawsuit claims that Truth API disadvantages regular investors, including individuals who hold investments via pension funds, 401(k) plans, and other retirement funds whose investment vehicles do not or cannot obtain Truth API’s early‑access information.

## How Truth PSI works
President Trump’s media company announced a new service, Truth PSI, that would let paying customers see certain Truth Social posts milliseconds before other users. The announcement stated that the service could include posts that might affect financial markets or national security, though the company said it had not clarified whether Trump’s own posts would be part of the feed. The company says that Truth PSI is part of a broader effort to generate new revenue.

## Why the early‑access model matters
Early access to presidential messages can provide investors with market‑moving information before it is publicly available. For example, on August 21 Trump announced a plan to lower the price of ground beef for working families by exempting some imports from tariffs. Such a policy announcement could influence commodity prices and related stocks within minutes of its release. The lawsuit argues that charging $100,000 a month to access these posts creates a marketplace that enables insider trading and gives wealthy institutions a disproportionate advantage over ordinary investors.

## Uncertainties and next steps
The lawsuit does not name President Trump as a defendant, but it points to his ownership stake and the potential conflict of interest in monetizing non‑public presidential information. The city attorney is urging a state court to block Trump’s social media platform Truth Social from offering customers who pay $100,000 per month priority access to the president’s and other high‑level officials’ posts before they’re available to other users. Until a court decision, the services remain available, and shareholders of Trump Media have seen a modest share price rise of 0.6 % to $9.63 following the announcement of Truth PSI.

## What to watch
Regulators and courts will decide whether early‑access services on political social‑media platforms violate state and federal ethics or securities laws. Investors and ordinary users should note that the current legal framework does not explicitly forbid paid pre‑release access, but the lawsuit raises the possibility that future enforcement could restrict such models. The outcome will influence how political content is monetized and how market participants can obtain timely information about government actions.
