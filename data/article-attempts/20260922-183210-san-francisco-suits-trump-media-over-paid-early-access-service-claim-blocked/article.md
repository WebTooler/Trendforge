## The lawsuit and what it alleges
San Francisco has filed a state‑court complaint against the Trump Media & Technology Group, the parent company of Truth Social, for selling early access to President Donald Trump’s posts on the platform. The suit claims that the company’s Truth API feed creates a “corrupt business scheme” that allows paying customers to view posts milliseconds before the general public, potentially giving them a market edge. The complaint also contends that the service is a form of insider trading and violates California anti‑corruption law.

## How the paid service works
Trump Media announced a new product called Truth PSI that would let subscribers see certain Truth Social posts before anyone else. The service is marketed to AI firms and large news organizations, and the company has not disclosed who its clients are. Critics argue that the service could let users trade on information that could move financial markets or affect national security. Trump Media said Truth PSI is part of a broader revenue effort and did not say whether the president’s own posts would be included in the early‑access feed.

## Who is at stake
The lawsuit highlights that Trump is the largest shareholder of Trump Media, owning more than 40 % of the company through a trust. While the former president is not named as a defendant, the suit argues that he is profiting from the private‑company marketplace that sells nonpublic information gained in his role as president. The city’s case also points to the broader market impact: Wall Street firms reportedly pay up to $100,000 a month for the service, and the company’s stock has fallen below $10 after briefly trading near $62 when it went public two years ago.

## What the evidence shows
Evidence shows that the city has sued for selling early access to Trump’s posts, that the company plans to market the service to AI and news clients, and that the service could give market‑moving information to subscribers. The lawsuit also notes the company’s high pricing for Wall Street firms and the fact that Trump owns a majority stake in the company. It further documents that the company’s stock has lost hundreds of millions of dollars each quarter and has dropped below $10 per share.

## What to watch next
The legal proceedings will clarify whether the service violates California law and whether the company can continue to charge for early access. The outcome may also influence how social‑media platforms handle presidential communications and whether similar services could be deemed illegal. Readers should monitor court filings for further details on client lists, pricing, and the company’s response to the accusations.
