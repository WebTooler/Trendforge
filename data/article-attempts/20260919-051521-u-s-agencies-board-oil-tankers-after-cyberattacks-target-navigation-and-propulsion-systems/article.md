## Incident Overview
Coast Guard personnel and FBI agents boarded two oil tankers last month after the vessels were hit by cyberattacks while heading toward the United States. The attacks targeted the ships’ networks and, in one case, interfered with propulsion, navigation and cargo systems, leaving the vessel’s communications offline for more than 30 hours.

## The Vessels and the Attacks
One of the ships was the VL Prosperity, a 1,093‑foot Liberian‑flagged crude oil tanker that was bound for Galveston, Texas. Iranian state media reported that hackers had accessed the ship’s propulsion, navigation and cargo systems and knocked out communications for a full day. The Coast Guard has not publicly attributed the attacks to any country.

## Boarding Operations
On August 21, a highly specialized team comprised of Coast Guard law‑enforcement officers, Coast Guard Cyber Protection Team members, a vessel inspector and FBI Cyber Action Team operators boarded the ship for four days. The boarding was carried out to ensure the integrity of the vessel’s operational and information‑technology systems after indications that the ship’s network had been compromised by foreign cyber actors.

## Findings and Current Status
Investigators have found evidence of a malicious cyber actor on board but have reported no operational disruptions, vessel instability, physical danger to crews, or environmental impacts. The crew cooperated with authorities and were considered critical partners in mitigating the threat. The Coast Guard and FBI are continuing their investigations into the incidents.

## Broader Maritime Context
The incident highlights the increasing vulnerability of shipping vessels as they adopt satellite communications and connect IT networks with onboard control systems. Because these ships face the internet, they present a potential vector for attackers to gain access and deploy malicious activity on the vessels.

## Industry and Government Response
Maritime industry experts emphasize that while all vessels should take the threat seriously, the federal government has imposed baseline cyber requirements across the sector. The incident underscores the need for vessel owners and operators to remain vigilant, as even a relatively small disruption could cause significant delays at U.S. ports, where an estimated $5.4 trillion in commerce flows annually.

## What to Watch
The Coast Guard and FBI are still investigating whether Iran or another actor was involved. No definitive attribution has been made public, and the agencies have not disclosed the exact nature of the attack beyond the fact that a malicious actor compromised the ships’ networks. Observers should monitor follow‑up statements from the agencies for additional details, including any evidence that could link the attacks to nation‑state actors or explain the attackers’ tactics, techniques and procedures.

## Conclusion
These events serve as a reminder that cyber threats to maritime shipping are real and can affect critical infrastructure. While the immediate incidents did not result in operational or environmental harm, they demonstrate how quickly a vessel’s systems can be compromised. Stakeholders in the shipping industry and port authorities should review their cyber defenses and coordinate with federal agencies to mitigate future risks.
