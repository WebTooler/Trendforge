## A Mole in the Hacker’s Core
In March, as the TeamPCP group began a wave of supply‑chain intrusions, Google’s own undercover analyst was invited to join its inner circle. The analyst, whose name has not been released, was one of about twelve members who gained access to the group’s core chat, dubbed CanisterWorm.

## From Intelligence to Action
Google’s team received a copy of the exploit code and tested it. With minor tweaks, the code worked—a rare instance of an in‑the‑wild AI‑created hacking technique that leveraged a previously unknown software vulnerability. The team warned the software’s developer, who patched the flaw. The incident was later described in a Google case study released in May, though the study did not name TeamPCP or detail how Google learned of the exploit.

## Partnering and Betrayal
TeamPCP had partnered with other criminal groups, most notably ShinyHunters, a prolific outfit that had extorted millions through data theft and ransomware. ShinyHunters first emerged online in late 2025 and had launched a series of cascading supply‑chain attacks that hid malware in open‑source software, hijacked developer credentials, and repeatedly planted malicious code in widely used tools. ShinyHunters eventually betrayed TeamPCP, taunting them on X and prompting the group’s attention.

## Disruption and Legal Follow‑up
Larsen, a Google threat‑intel lead, said his goal was to disrupt the campaign before more compromises occurred. Google’s Cyber Disruption Unit, launched around the same time, officially tasked the company with a more aggressive stance against cybercrime. Instead of alerting each breached company, Google reached out to providers where stolen credentials could be used—such as Amazon Web Services and Microsoft—to revoke access and prevent further exploitation. The team sent hundreds of notification emails, many of which received immediate responses.

## Arrest and Aftermath
About a month after Google’s tip, U.S. law enforcement completed a warrant‑based request for data from Google. The next month, Australian police arrested the group’s alleged leader, Thomson, and released a video of him being escorted out of a suburban home.

## What Readers Should Watch
Google’s infiltration demonstrates a shift toward proactive disruption rather than reactive notification. The success of the operation hinges on close cooperation between threat‑intel teams and cloud providers. Future cases may see more analysts embedded in criminal networks, but the legal and operational challenges—such as obtaining warrants and coordinating across jurisdictions—remain significant hurdles for the industry.
