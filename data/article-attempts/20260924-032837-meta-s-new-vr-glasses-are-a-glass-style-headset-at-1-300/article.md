## Meta Unveils a Glass‑Style VR Experience
At its Connect event, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg announced a next‑generation VR headset that looks like a pair of oversized sunglasses. The device, named the Meta VR Glasses, will ship in Spring 2027 and will be sold for $1,300. The glasses are tethered to an external computing puck that can fit in a pocket.

## Design and Hardware
The glasses themselves are a lightweight frame that sits on the ears. They use six external cameras for color passthrough and motion tracking, plus four internal cameras for eye tracking. The detachable puck contains a Snapdragon Reality Elite processor, 12 GB of RAM, 128 GB of storage, a microSD slot, and a battery that delivers up to three hours of media playback. While the puck is charging, the glasses remain usable.

The display is a 5K MicroOLED “Infinite Display.” Each eye receives 2,412 × 2,288 pixels, which translates to 37 pixels per degree, and the system can drive a 120 Hz refresh rate.

## Software and Content
Meta says the glasses run Quest OS, the same operating system that powers the Quest 3. This means that 3‑D content built for Quest could eventually be available on the glasses. Meta also announced a “VR Enhanced Cinema” format that lets users watch 3‑D movies with effects that bleed into the real world, similar to ambient lighting on TVs.

In a demo, Meta showed footage from James Cameron’s Billie Eilish concert film, a VR180 NBA Finals clip, and a “VR Enhanced Cinema” version of Avengers: Endgame produced with Disney and Industrial Light & Magic.

## Position in the Market
The $1,300 price places the glasses between Meta’s $599.99 Quest 3 and Apple’s Vision Pro, which retails for $3,499. Meta markets the glasses as an all‑in‑one entertainment and productivity device.

## Privacy and Public Use
Because the glasses rely on external cameras for passthrough and tracking, they do not carry the same privacy concerns that Meta’s Ray‑Ban and Display glasses had. However, Meta has already stated that it believes wearing cameras in public is problematic.

## What’s Still Unknown
Meta has not yet confirmed whether the glasses will support standalone use, the full range of Quest applications, or future content distribution. The company has also said that it will continue developing full VR headsets, indicating that the glasses are a complementary product rather than a replacement.

## Takeaway
Meta’s glasses bring a headset‑like immersion into a glasses‑style form factor. With a tethered puck, 5K OLED panels, and Quest OS compatibility, they offer a lower‑priced alternative to Vision Pro while maintaining a comparable visual experience. Users who want immersive VR without a bulky headset may find the $1,300 price point attractive, but the glasses’ reliance on an external puck and the lack of standalone support may limit their appeal for some.

What the Evidence Shows
Meta’s VR Glasses are a $1,300, glasses‑style device that pairs with a pocket‑size puck containing a Snapdragon Reality Elite processor, 12 GB of RAM, 128 GB of storage, and a 3‑hour battery, runs Quest OS, and supports a 5K MicroOLED display with 120 Hz refresh.
