## The narrowing gap between open and closed frontier models
Ars Technica previewed a Mozilla report that documents how inexpensive open‑weight models have closed the performance gap with leading closed frontier AI. According to the analysis, the best Chinese open models now sit just 4.4 months behind the top U.S. frontier systems. The State of Open Source AI report, published on September 15, 2026, recommends that most organizations default to open models for the bulk of their workloads.

## Cost calculus: when paying for frontier pays off
Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3, an open model, scores only three points below Anthropic’s closed Fable 5 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index while costing about 30 % of Fable’s price. Raffi Krikorian, Mozilla’s chief technology officer, explained in an email to Ars that “[A Closed model] earns its premium in a few places: expert professional work, high‑intensity retrieval, and long context.” Organizations still buy closed frontier models because they come bundled with compliance packaging, support, and accountability, and many firms lack the staff to run open‑weight models effectively.

Krikorian’s guidance frames the decision: “Pay when that head start is worth it, something like a deadline that lands before the open frontier catches up would be here.” Conversely, he added, “Routine work you’ll still be doing next quarter is not, because you’ll be able to do it for a fifth of the cost soon, and the model won’t be the bottleneck anyway.” The report calculates that paying for a closed frontier model provides roughly a four‑month performance lead at about five times the per‑task cost, but only for workloads lasting eight to twelve hours.

## Practical implications for enterprises
DoorDash illustrates the shift, using Kimi for routine tasks and reserving Fable for more complex work that would otherwise require human experts. Since Mozilla’s inaugural State of Open Source AI report on July 14, 2026, adoption of open models has accelerated. OpenRouter data shows eight of the ten most‑used models by token volume in August 2026 are open‑weight, highlighting a clear preference among developers. Despite this surge, open models still generate far less revenue than their closed counterparts.

## What remains uncertain and what to watch
The evidence does not specify how the four‑month gap will evolve beyond the current measurement, nor does it cover all possible task categories. It also leaves open the exact revenue disparity and the long‑term impact of compliance and support differences. Readers should monitor upcoming benchmark updates, cost trends for both open and closed models, and any new open releases that could further shrink the performance gap. Tracking adoption metrics on platforms like OpenRouter and watching for Mozilla’s next State of Open Source AI report will provide the clearest signals of where the market is heading.
